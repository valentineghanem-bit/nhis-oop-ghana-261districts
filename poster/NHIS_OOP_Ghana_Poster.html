Background & Objectives

Ghana's NHIS (Act 650, 2003) aims to eliminate catastrophic OOP expenditure. Despite enrolment efforts, uninsurance rates remain highly heterogeneous (range: 5.2–73.2%), with spatial clustering indicating structural inequities beyond individual-level factors. This study identifies district-level determinants and delineates high-risk geographic clusters using spatial epidemiology and ML.

Objectives: (1) Quantify spatial autocorrelation; (2) identify predictor co-clustering; (3) delineate hotspot districts; (4) develop ML risk models with SHAP interpretability.

Data Sources

Outcome: Uninsured population — PHC 2021 (GSS).
Determinants: Poverty, illiteracy, youth dependency, employment — PHC 2021.
NHIS coverage: DHS 2019/2022.
OOP trends: WHO GHO 2000–2022.
Boundaries: Ghana MMDA shapefile, 261 districts.

Ethics

All data are publicly available aggregated records. No individual-level data accessed. Ecological analysis — GHS ethics board approval not required. STROBE reporting standards applied throughout.

Discussion & Conclusions

Spatial autocorrelation (I=0.461, p<0.001) confirms non-random NHIS non-coverage clustering. HH clusters in the Northern/Oti belts reflect poverty and geographic access barriers. Poverty incidence dominates all ML models; RF achieves AUC=0.918. Spatially targeted premium-waiver schemes and community outreach are warranted for high-burden Northern belt districts.
